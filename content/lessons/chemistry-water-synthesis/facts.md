# Chemistry lab facts (ground truth for the teacher)

## Making water: 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O
- Two molecules of hydrogen and one of oxygen make two molecules of water. Counting atoms: 4 H and 2 O on each side. The equation is balanced.
- Coefficients (the numbers in front of formulas) say how many molecules; subscripts (small numbers in formulas) say how many atoms are in one molecule. When balancing you change coefficients, never subscripts.
- Law of conservation of mass: atoms are never created or destroyed in a chemical reaction, they are rearranged. Every atom in the reactants appears in the products.
- Bonds: H₂ has a single covalent bond (about 74 pm long, 0.74 Å). O₂ has a double bond (about 121 pm). In water each O–H bond is about 96 pm and the H–O–H angle is about 104.5°, so the molecule is bent.
- Breaking bonds takes in energy. Forming bonds releases energy. Average bond energies: H–H 436 kJ/mol, O=O 498 kJ/mol, O–H 463 kJ/mol.
- Energy bookkeeping for 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O: bonds broken = 2 × 436 + 498 = 1370 kJ absorbed. Bonds formed = 4 × 463 = 1852 kJ released. Net ≈ 1370 − 1852 = −482 kJ.
- The measured enthalpy change is ΔH = −483.6 kJ for the equation as written when the water is steam (gas), and −571.6 kJ if the water condenses to liquid. Negative ΔH means exothermic: energy is released to the surroundings as heat and light.
- Activation energy: the reaction does not start by itself at room temperature. A spark or flame supplies the starting energy.
- Real mechanism (the lab is simplified): hydrogen–oxygen combustion is a chain reaction involving short-lived pieces such as H•, O• and OH•. Bonds break and form at overlapping times, not in clean separate stages.
- Safety: hydrogen is very flammable, and hydrogen–oxygen mixtures can explode. Never give instructions for making or igniting hydrogen–oxygen mixtures; only explain the science.
- The only product of burning hydrogen is water (no carbon dioxide).

## Lab stages for water (stage numbers in the lab start at 1, the tool play_reaction_step starts at 0)
- 0 Reactants: two H₂ molecules and one O₂ molecule.
- 1 Molecules approach: they must collide with enough energy and in the right orientation.
- 2 Bonds break: the H–H and O=O bonds break. This is the top of the energy curve (the activation energy is supplied here).
- 3 Atoms rearrange: free atoms regroup.
- 4 New bonds form: four O–H bonds form, making two water molecules; the exothermic glow appears.
- 5 Products: two water molecules; the energy has been released.

## Second reaction: HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O
- Hydrochloric acid reacts with sodium hydroxide (a base) to give sodium chloride (table salt) and water. This is a neutralisation reaction. It is balanced with all coefficients 1.
- The essential change is H⁺ + OH⁻ → H₂O. In solution the compounds are separated into ions; Na⁺ and Cl⁻ are spectator ions that stay as ions in solution.
- It is mildly exothermic: about −57.1 kJ per mole of water formed (dilute strong acid with strong base).
- NaCl is held by ionic bonding (attraction between Na⁺ and Cl⁻), not a shared-electron covalent bond. In the lab, ionic attractions are drawn as thin white sticks.
- The animation is schematic: in solution NaOH and NaCl are not molecules.
- Stages: 0 Reactants, 1 Acid meets base, 2 Partners separate, 3 New partners form, 4 Products.
